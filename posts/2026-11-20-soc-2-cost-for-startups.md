**SOC 2 cost for a startup is the auditor's fee plus everything needed to pass: compliance tooling, readiness and implementation work, a penetration test and your team's time.** The audit fee is often the smallest predictable line; tooling and internal effort are where budgets quietly double. Scope tightly, right-size your tools and keep controls running, and a first report becomes far more affordable.

## What makes up the cost of SOC 2?

| Cost component | What it covers | How to keep it down |
|---|---|---|
| CPA audit fee | The independent examination and report | Narrow scope; get several quotes |
| Compliance tooling | Evidence collection, policy templates, dashboards | Buy the tier that fits your headcount |
| Readiness and implementation | Scoping, gap fixing, policies, audit support | Do a readiness assessment first |
| Penetration test | Expected by most auditors and buyers | Scope it to your real attack surface |
| Security tools you're missing | SSO/MFA, logging, endpoint, backups | Use what your cloud already provides |
| Internal team time | Engineers and leaders implementing and evidencing | Assign owners and automate evidence |

## How much is the SOC 2 audit fee?

Audit fees vary with scope, report type, company size and the audit firm. Published estimates for small companies commonly fall around **$5,000–25,000 for a Type I** and **$7,000–50,000 or more for a Type II**, with large or complex environments costing much more. Treat any single number with caution — the only reliable figure is a quote based on your scope.

What moves the fee:

- **Type I vs Type II** — Type II tests controls over a period, so it costs more. See [SOC 2 Type I vs Type II](/blog/soc-2-type-1-vs-type-2/).
- **Trust Services Criteria** — each criterion beyond Security adds testing. See [which criteria you need](/blog/soc-2-trust-services-criteria/).
- **Number of systems and locations** in scope
- **Readiness** — a messy environment means more auditor hours and more exceptions

## What do compliance tools cost?

Compliance automation platforms are usually sold as annual subscriptions, often tiered by headcount and number of frameworks. They save real time on evidence collection, but they're also where startups most often overspend: buying an enterprise tier, or a multi-framework bundle, before they need it. ## What do readiness and implementation cost?

This is the work that actually gets you through the audit: scoping, fixing cloud and access controls, writing policies that match how you work, and preparing evidence. You can do it in-house if you have experienced security staff, or pay a consultant on a project or monthly basis. Either way, start with a [SOC 2 readiness assessment](/blog/soc-2-readiness-assessment/) so you know the size of the job before committing to a budget.

## What about the hidden cost: your team's time?

Internal time is the line nobody budgets for. Engineers fix configurations, managers run access reviews, someone chases evidence and answers auditor questions. If the work isn't planned, it competes with product work for months. Clear control owners and automated evidence collection cut this significantly. See [how long SOC 2 takes](/blog/how-long-does-soc-2-take/).

## How can a startup reduce SOC 2 cost safely?

1. **Scope to Security only** for your first report unless a customer requires more.
2. **Get readiness done before the audit** so you don't pay auditors to find basic gaps.
3. **Right-size your tooling** to your headcount and the frameworks you'll use this year.
4. **Use your cloud provider's native security services** before buying new tools.
5. **Choose a short first Type II window** where buyers accept it.
6. **Keep controls running all year** so the renewal is routine, not a rebuild.
7. **Get two or three audit quotes** with the same scope description.

## Year one vs year two

Year one carries one-off costs: readiness, remediation, policies and setup. Year two should be cheaper — but only if access reviews, scans, backups and evidence collection kept running. Companies that stop after the first report often pay for a second round of remediation at renewal. We regularly meet startups paying $20,000–30,000 a year for a compliance automation platform before they've fixed a single control. In our experience, the same audit outcome is often achievable for 20–30% of that tooling spend by choosing tools that fit the company's size and doing the implementation work properly. The other big saving is continuity: when controls keep running between audits, the renewal is a formality instead of a new project.

## FAQ

### How much does SOC 2 cost for a small startup?

It depends on scope, report type and how much security is already in place. Add up the audit fee, tooling, readiness or consulting help, a penetration test and internal time — and get an audit quote based on your actual scope.

### Is SOC 2 Type II more expensive than Type I?

Yes. Type II tests that controls operated over a period, so the audit takes more work. Many startups still go straight to Type II with a short first window to avoid paying for two audits.

### Do I need a penetration test for SOC 2?

SOC 2 doesn't name a penetration test as a strict requirement, but most auditors and enterprise buyers expect one as evidence of vulnerability management.

### Is SOC 2 cheaper in the second year?

Usually, because one-off readiness work is done. The saving only holds if controls kept operating between audits.
