**ISO/IEC 42001 certification proves that your company runs a managed, auditable AI Management System (AIMS).** Getting there means scoping your AI systems, building the management-system processes the standard requires, running them for long enough to produce evidence, and passing a two-stage audit by an accredited certification body. Companies with an existing SOC 2 or ISO 27001 program move noticeably faster, because much of the operational groundwork already exists.

## The steps to certification

### 1. Scope

Decide which AI systems, products and teams are covered, and your role for each — developer, provider or user of AI. A tight, honest scope is faster to certify and easier to maintain.

### 2. Gap assessment

Compare where you are today against Clauses 4–10 and the Annex A controls. The output is a prioritised remediation plan.

### 3. Build the AIMS

- AI policy and objectives, approved by leadership
- Roles and responsibilities for AI
- AI risk assessment and risk treatment methodology
- **AI system impact assessments** — effects on individuals, groups and society
- Statement of Applicability (which Annex A controls apply, and why)
- AI lifecycle processes: design, data, development, testing, deployment, monitoring
- Third-party / supplier processes for AI

### 4. Operate it

Run the processes for real: complete risk and impact assessments, train staff, monitor AI systems, handle issues. Certification auditors want to see the system **working**, not just documented.

### 5. Internal audit and management review

Both are mandatory before certification. The internal audit checks the AIMS against the standard; management review shows leadership is engaged and acting on results.

### 6. Certification audit

- **Stage 1** — documentation review and readiness check
- **Stage 2** — evidence that the AIMS is implemented and effective

Nonconformities found at Stage 2 must be corrected before the certificate is issued.

### 7. Maintain it

Certificates follow a three-year cycle with **surveillance audits** in the intervening years and a recertification audit at the end.

## Typical timeline

| Starting point | Typical path to Stage 2 |
|---|---|
| Existing SOC 2 or ISO 27001, few AI systems | Roughly 3–6 months |
| Some security program, several AI systems | Roughly 6–9 months |
| No formal management system | 9–12 months or more |

These are planning ranges, not promises — the biggest variables are scope, how many AI systems you run, and how quickly your team can complete assessments.

## What drives cost

There's no fixed price. Total cost is made up of:

- **Certification body fees** — driven by scope, number of sites and headcount
- **Implementation effort** — internal time plus any consulting support
- **Tooling** — often none needed beyond what you use for SOC 2
- **Remediation** — technical controls you don't yet have (monitoring, logging, data governance)
- **Ongoing cost** — surveillance audits, internal audits and keeping assessments current

The cheapest path is usually to **reuse your SOC 2 controls**, keep scope tight, and avoid buying new platforms until you know you need them.

## How SOC 2 work speeds things up

Risk assessment, access control, change management, supplier management, incident response, logging, training and internal oversight already exist in a mature SOC 2 program. The new work is mainly AI-specific: impact assessments, AI lifecycle controls, data quality and provenance, transparency and human oversight. See [SOC 2 vs ISO 42001](/blog/soc-2-vs-iso-42001/).

## FAQ

### Is ISO 42001 mandatory?

No. It's voluntary. Companies pursue it because customers ask for it, or to demonstrate structured AI governance alongside regulation such as the [EU AI Act](/blog/eu-ai-act-for-us-saas-companies/).

### Does ISO 42001 certification mean we comply with the EU AI Act?

No. It provides a strong management framework that supports compliance, but legal obligations under the Act must be assessed separately.

### Can a small startup get certified?

Yes. The standard scales — a small company with a tight scope and a few AI systems can certify without heavy bureaucracy.
