**The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) is a free, voluntary framework from the US National Institute of Standards and Technology for managing risks across the AI lifecycle.** It is organised into four functions — Govern, Map, Measure and Manage — and it is the most practical starting point for most companies building AI governance. There is no NIST AI RMF certification; you use it to build and evidence a risk practice.

## What are the four functions of the NIST AI RMF?

### Govern

Culture, policies, roles and accountability for AI risk. This is cross-cutting — it informs the other three.

### Map

Understand context: what each AI system does, who it affects, which data it uses and what could go wrong.

### Measure

Analyze and track identified risks with quantitative and qualitative methods — testing, evaluation, monitoring.

### Manage

Prioritize and act on risks: treat, transfer, accept or avoid, and respond to incidents.

## What is the NIST Generative AI Profile?

NIST published a companion Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1) in July 2024. It highlights risks such as confabulation, data privacy, information security and harmful content, with suggested actions mapped to the four functions.

## How does a SaaS company apply the NIST AI RMF?

1. Build an inventory of AI systems and owners.
2. Assess each system against the four functions.
3. Prioritize gaps by business risk and customer demand.
4. Map resulting controls into your SOC 2 program.

## FAQ

### Is the NIST AI RMF mandatory?

No. It is voluntary guidance, though some customers and public-sector buyers use it as their reference for how AI risk should be managed.

### Can you get NIST AI RMF certified?

No. There is no official certification. You can be assessed against it and share the results with customers, and you can formalise the same work into ISO/IEC 42001, which is certifiable.

### How does the NIST AI RMF relate to SOC 2?

SOC 2 has no AI-specific criteria. The NIST AI RMF fills that gap: the risks and controls it identifies can be added to your SOC 2 risk assessment, vendor reviews and change management.
